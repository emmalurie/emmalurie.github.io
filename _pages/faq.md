## Research-related

 If you’re considering joining the lab, I hope this helps you figure out whether it would be a good fit.
---

## My Approach to Mentorship

I think of research as something you learn by doing, not something you already know how to do when you start.

My goal is to help you:
- learn how to ask good questions  
- develop your ideas  
- become a stronger writer and researcher  

That means I will:
- give direct, detailed feedback  
- ask you to revise your work  
- push you to clarify your thinking  

You are not expected to know everything when you start. You are expected to engage seriously and improve over time.

---

## What I Value

The students who tend to do well working with me are:
- **Curious** — you care about the ideas, not just finishing tasks  
- **Thoughtful** — you think about the broader implications of your work  
- **Communicative** — you keep me updated and ask questions  
- **Reliable** — you follow through on what you commit to  

---

## How I Work

We will usually:
- meet weekly (lab + 1:1)  
- work iteratively over time  
- use writing as a way to think  

I will often:
- suggest readings
- leave detailed comments on drafts  
- ask you to explain or rethink parts of your work  

---

## Communication

A few norms that make things run smoothly:

- Try to respond within ~24 hours on weekdays  
- Let me know early if something changes or you’re falling behind  
- If you’re stuck, reach out  

You do not need to have everything figured out before asking a question—it’s usually better to ask earlier.

---

## Feedback

My feedback style is direct.

This means:
- I will point out what is unclear or not working  
- I may ask you to redo something  

This is not a judgment of you—it’s how research works. Revision is a normal (and important) part of the process.

---

## Independence

Over time, I will expect you to take more ownership of your work.

That can look like:
- proposing next steps  
- shaping the direction of your project  
- making decisions about what to prioritize  

You don’t need to start here—but this is where we’re going.

---

## Boundaries

- I do not expect work outside of reasonable hours  
- I may not respond at night or on weekends  
- If you are overwhelmed or falling behind, please tell me early  

It is always better to communicate than to disappear.

---

## Fit

This is likely a good fit if you:
- are interested in technology and society  
- are comfortable with some ambiguity  
- want to improve your writing and thinking  

This may not be a good fit if you:
- want very step-by-step instructions for every task  
- are only interested in building products without analysis  
- are not able to commit consistent time  

---

## Eligibility and Structure

I primarily work with **Barnard and Columbia undergraduates**, as well as **Columbia master’s students**.

During the academic year, most students work with me **for course credit** (typically through independent study at Barnard or Columbia), though I may also have a small number of **paid research assistant positions** depending on project needs and available funding. Over the summer, roles are typically **paid and full-time**.

I do not offer volunteer positions, and I am not able to work with high school students.

If you are a student at another institution and have your own funding (for example, through a fellowship or grant), feel free to reach out—I am sometimes able to support external collaborators.

I also occasionally have limited funding to support a **graduate-level researcher** over the summer. If you are a graduate student and interested in this type of role, you are welcome to get in touch.

---

## How to Reach Out

If you’re a Barnard or Columbia student and are interested in working with me, feel free to email me at **XXX@barnard.edu**.

A short message introducing yourself and your interests is more than enough—I’m happy to talk through whether there’s a good fit.

---

## Final Note

You do not need prior research experience to work with me.

Many students I work with are doing research for the first time. What matters is that you show up, engage seriously, and are willing to learn.
